# Official guidance consulted on November 21, 2026

- Google Search Central recommends consistent canonical signals across canonical tags, redirects, sitemaps, and links. Its AI Search guidance uses standard search eligibility and people-first SEO practices. [Canonicalization](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls) [AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)
- Google documents BlogPosting article fields and recommends representative images. Schema.org defines BlogPosting for blog posts. Valid structured data supports interpretation. Actual rich-result display remains a provider decision. [Google Article](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article) [Schema.org BlogPosting](https://schema.org/BlogPosting)
- Bing documents sitemaps and IndexNow as discovery and change-notification mechanisms. Submitting a URL does not guarantee indexing. [Bing sitemaps](https://www4.bing.com/webmasters/help/sitemaps-3b5cf6ed) [IndexNow](https://www.bing.com/indexnow/getstarted)
- OpenAI distinguishes OAI-SearchBot, GPTBot, and user-triggered ChatGPT-User. Anthropic documents separate search, user, and model-development bots. Perplexity distinguishes search discovery from user-triggered retrieval. The providers publish different access guidance. [OpenAI crawlers](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) [Anthropic crawlers](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8896518) [Perplexity crawlers](https://docs.perplexity.ai/docs/resources/perplexity-crawlers)
- Chrome recommends early HTML discovery, high fetch priority, and eager loading for an image that is the LCP element. [Chrome LCP request discovery](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/performance/insights/lcp-discovery) [Web.dev LCP optimization](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-lcp)
- The site publishes llms.txt and static WebMCP discovery as experimental integrations. Crawl and answer eligibility were assessed independently of those integrations.
